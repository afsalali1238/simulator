CH. 1

The Big Picture

Build one mental model, then hang everything off it.

Start with the "why" — it changes everything

Most people assume a GPS system exists to show dots moving on a map. For Dozr, that's not the point. The reason this system exists is:

The north star

It produces a billing-grade utilisation number that survives a dispute — not a map with dots on it.

Picture the argument the system has to win. You rent an excavator to a contractor for a month, then invoice: "It ran 143 hours — here's the bill." The contractor says, "No it didn't, it sat idle half the time." Your system's job is to produce a record so trustworthy that the customer pays — and that would hold up if it ever reached a lawyer. The live map, the alerts, the fuel charts are all bonuses sitting on top of that one hard number. This is why the "boring" parts (never losing data, never counting twice) matter more than the flashy map.

What "telematics" even means

The word is just telecommunications + informatics — "machine information, sent from far away." Two levels:

GPS tracking answers one question: where is it?  Telematics answers two: where is it, and what is it doing? — engine on or off, hours run, fuel level, moving or still, being towed away. Dozr does the second one, and that "what is it doing" is where the money is, because hours run is what you bill.

The system in four layers

Imagine strapping a screenless smartphone to an excavator — one that does a single job: report on the machine. That "phone," and everything behind it, breaks into four layers. Data flows left to right.

Watch one "ping" travel the whole way

A single location report — the industry calls it a ping or a record — from an excavator on a Dubai site to your screen:

  1. The excavator's engine turns on. The Teltonika box notices ignition went off→on and grabs its GPS position, the time, and "engine running."
  2. It bundles that into a small message — in Teltonika's own language, called Codec 8 — and sends it out through the SIM over 4G.
  3. The message crosses the cellular network and the internet and arrives at your backend.
  4. The backend decodes it and writes the record safely to the database before it says "got it" (an "ACK"). This order is sacred: the device deletes its only copy the instant it hears "got it," so ACK-before-save means the data is gone forever. (Invariant #1 in your docs — now you know why.)
  5. If the box never hears "got it" (signal dropped), it re-sends later — so the backend must recognise "I've seen this exact record before" and not count it twice. Double-counted hours = an overbill you'll lose in a dispute.
  6. The record lands in the database. The dashboard reads from there: the dot updates, the hours tick up, and that number rolls toward the month-end invoice.

That sequence is the system. Every feature is a variation on it.

Where AWS actually fits

AWS (Amazon Web Services) is just Amazon renting you always-on computers in their data centers instead of you buying and babysitting your own. Layer 3 (and often Layer 4) has to run somewhere 24/7 — AWS is one place to run it.

The nuance to walk in with

"Using AWS" can mean two different things. One is simply renting the computers — and AWS has a data-center region in the UAE (me-central-1), which matters if your data must legally stay in-country. The other is building on AWS's specialised "IoT" products (services named things like IoT Core) that promise to catch the device data for you. Your team's current recommendation deliberately avoids those heavy IoT products for the pilot and uses a lean, ready-made open-source tool called Traccar instead — it already speaks Teltonika on day one and is far less to build and run.

So both can be true: you might host on AWS (in Dubai) but not build on AWS's IoT machinery. This is a real decision you own — logged as D3 in the PRD — not settled fact.

The two jobs this serves

Utilisation / billing — how much did the machine actually run? The money job: rental invoices, "is this asset earning its keep," ROI.  Logistics / movement — where is it, is it on the right site, did it leave the yard, when will this load arrive? The operations job — and the one that tends to get under-served in specs, so keep an eye on it as the lead.

The words your team will use

Telematics
Location plus what the machine is doing (engine, hours, fuel). More than "GPS tracking."
Ping / record
One report from a device: position, time, and status at a moment.
Teltonika
The brand of GPS/telematics device Dozr uses. The box on the machine.
Codec 8 / 8E
The "language" (data format) Teltonika devices speak when they send records.
ACK
The backend's "got it" reply. Must come only after the record is safely saved.
CAN bus
The machine's internal network the device can tap to read true engine data (like real engine hours).
Utilisation
How much a machine was actually used — the number you bill on.
Traccar
Ready-made open-source software that catches Teltonika data. The "buy, don't build" option for the pilot.
Supabase
The toolkit for the app layer (login, database, security, APIs). Already used by Dozr's other products.
fleet-v2
Your existing dashboard — 9 finished screens on mock data, waiting to be fed real data.
me-central-1
AWS's UAE data-center region — relevant if data must stay in-country.
Questions that make you sound sharp
  • "When we say using AWS — do we mean hosting what we build, or building on AWS's IoT services? Those are different bills and different amounts of work."
  • "Where does a record get durably saved before we ACK the device? Walk me through what happens if the server dies mid-stream."
  • "How do we stop a re-sent buffer from double-counting hours?"
  • "For the pilot, what does Traccar give us for free, and what's the first thing that would force us off it?"
  • "Are we serving logistics/movement as seriously as utilisation/billing, or is that under-built right now?"
