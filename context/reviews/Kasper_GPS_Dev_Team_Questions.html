Dev Team Engagement Pack

Questions to ask about the five-month, nine-person estimate — and concrete suggestions for doing it faster and with less risk, given that we own the hardware and they own the software.

00The split, and what it changes

We deal with Teltonika, procurement, SIMs, installation and everything physical. They build the software. That division is sensible and it should be stated explicitly in both plans, because two things follow from it that neither side has written down yet.

Their estimate is now software-only

Nine people for five months is roughly 45 person-months of software, with no hardware, procurement, installation or field work inside it. That makes the number harder to justify, not easier — the excluded work is real work, and it has been excluded.

The risk moves to the seam

When one team owns the devices and another owns the code, the most likely cause of a slip is a handoff nobody dated. They cannot test against real traffic until we supply a device; we cannot verify an install until they supply a listener. Neither plan currently mentions the other.

The productive framing

Do not open with “why so long, why so many people”. Open with “here is our side of the plan — what do you need from us, and when?” It is a genuine question, it is the one most likely to be under-thought on their side, and the answer tells you immediately whether they have understood that this system involves physical machines. A team that says “we'll need two devices on a public endpoint by week three” has thought about it. A team that says “nothing, we'll mock the data” has not.

01How to run the conversation

  • Ask for the breakdown before challenging the total. Any credible five-month estimate can be decomposed into person-months per area. Asking for it is normal and non-confrontational — and it either produces a reasoned answer or reveals that the number came from a team template.
  • Do not present a counter-number as a target. “We think this is closer to two engineers” invites a defensive re-quote. “Here is what we think the work is — what do you see that we don't?” invites engineering.
  • Separate the estimate from the team. The estimate may be wrong and the team may still be right for the job. Keep those two judgements apart in the meeting or you will end up arguing about people.
  • Ask what would make it faster. Directly: “what would you cut, and what would you need from us, to have something real running in eight weeks?” Good teams enjoy this question.
  • Take notes on what they ask you. The questions a team asks about devices, CAN data, buffering and replay tell you more about their fit than any answer they give about timelines.

02The three that matter most

If the meeting is short, ask only these.

1  ·  Can you break the five months into person-months by area?
Tests whether the estimate is derived or assumed

Ingestion and protocol handling, data model and access control, the dashboard screens, reporting, infrastructure, testing. Roughly, not to the day.

Why it matters: 45 person-months has to land somewhere. If ingestion is 4 and screens are 25, they have misjudged where the difficulty is. If they cannot produce the split at all, the number came from a standard team shape rather than from this work.

2  ·  What do you need from us, and by when?
The handoffs neither plan has dated

Devices, SIMs, a machine to fit an adapter to, the list of which machines can produce engine hours, access to the existing screens and specification.

Why it matters: this is the seam where the schedule will actually break, and it is the question their estimate has not been forced to confront. It also converts the meeting from a negotiation into joint planning.

3  ·  Are you writing a Codec 8E parser, or adopting one?
Potentially four to six weeks and most of the protocol risk

The devices speak Teltonika's binary format over raw TCP — there is no MQTT and no HTTPS. Something has to terminate that connection, complete the IMEI handshake, validate the checksum and return the acknowledgement. Open-source servers already do this in production.

Why it matters: if they are writing it from scratch, ask what that buys over adopting a tested one. There are legitimate answers — control over when the acknowledgement is sent is the main one — but it should be a decision with a reason, not a default.

03The estimate

Which five workstreams are the five developers on, and what are the interfaces between them?
Tests whether the work actually parallelises

The core of this system is one telemetry record shape, one schema, one tenancy model and one ingestion path. Those are one- or two-person artefacts by nature.

If the answer is five feature areas that all touch the same schema, the developers will spend a material share of their time coordinating rather than building.

What are the three assumptions that, if wrong, break the five months?
Every real estimate has them; a template has none

A team that answers this quickly and specifically has stress-tested its own number. A team that says “we've allowed contingency” has not.

Does the five months start at signature, or when the team is complete?
Potentially two to three months of difference

“Recruiting experienced talent will be necessary” means the clock cannot start until people are hired, have served notice, and have started. Ask who carries the schedule risk if hiring runs long, and what the plan is if only part of the team materialises.

What does the first month cost, and what do we own at the end of it?
Makes a phased commitment possible

Whatever the total, the first month should be separately priced and separately valuable. It is also the cheapest way for both sides to find out whether this partnership works.

04Scope and reuse

What does “the initial version” contain? In writing.
The answer changes the meaning of every other answer

Specifically: which screens, which user types, whether there is a customer-facing surface, whether there is a public API, whether multi-tenancy is in or out.

If their scope is substantially larger than ours, the team size may be defensible and the real problem is that nobody has written the scope down.

Have you seen the seven dashboard screens that already exist, and the architecture and protocol documents?
Determines whether they are scoping greenfield

A working prototype of the dashboard exists, on our brand, with the data shapes already modelled. The device and protocol specification is written. The architecture reference is current.

If the month-one display POC is rebuilding something we already have, that is the clearest single saving available.

What in the proposal is genuinely new build, versus integration of things that exist?
Separates the real work from the restated work

Wiring existing screens to live data is integration and it is a fraction of the cost of designing and building them.

05Technical approach

What is the DevOps engineer deploying?
A full-time infrastructure role implies an architecture decision already taken

A viable pilot for this can be a single server and a database. If the plan involves a load balancer, a container fleet, a stream and several managed stores, that is a decision worth examining before it is staffed — it is defensible at scale and heavy at ten devices.

When the server acknowledges a batch, has it already been durably stored?
The single most important technical question in the system

The device deletes its only copy of a record the moment it is acknowledged. Acknowledging before the write is confirmed means a crash loses data silently — and this data is what a utilisation record is built from.

How they answer tells you whether they have thought about the device as a system with its own state, or as a client that posts JSON.

How do you attribute a record to a customer when machines change hands mid-hire?
Tests whether they have understood the tenancy problem

An asset works for one customer this quarter and another next. If tenancy is a column on the asset, re-hiring either rewrites history or exposes the previous customer's data.

The right answer involves dated assignment windows and resolving at the record's own timestamp. “We'll add a tenant ID” is the answer that costs a rebuild later.

What happens when a device reconnects after four hours offline?
Replay is routine here, not an edge case

It replays thousands of buffered records with timestamps hours old, interleaved with live ones. Trips, engine-hour deltas and geofence events all have to come out the same regardless of arrival order — and stale events must not fire alerts.

07Team and hiring

Can you start with a smaller core and ramp on evidence?
Reduces their hiring risk and our exposure at the same time

An architect and two engineers can deliver a thin end-to-end slice. Hiring continues in parallel, and the team grows when there is work that genuinely parallelises — which is later than the plan assumes.

This is a suggestion that helps them. Committing to nine people from day one means they must hire nine people before they can start earning, and any hiring slip is their problem and our delay.

Who specifically is on this, and have they built device telemetry before?
The most predictive question about outcome

Not CVs — one concrete example of a system with real devices, buffering, replay and a binary protocol. Web application experience does not transfer to the parts of this that are hard.

What happens if only five of the nine are hired by the start date?
Tests whether the plan degrades gracefully

The answer reveals whether the estimate is a real plan or a resourcing formula.

08Quality

How do you generate device traffic before hardware exists?
The answer that unblocks their whole schedule

The frame format is fully documented, so valid test packets can be constructed without a device. A traffic generator lets them work at full speed while our procurement runs, and it is the only way to test outage replay, duplicate delivery and clock skew on demand.

If they have not planned one, suggesting it is the single most useful thing you can offer them.

What is your test approach for a four-hour outage replay?
Separates telemetry experience from web experience

This cannot be tested by clicking through screens. It needs generated data with deliberately awkward properties: out-of-order timestamps, duplicates, gaps, implausible clocks.

What are the two QA engineers testing in month two?
A fair question, not a hostile one

Before there is a device, a user or a screen on live data, it is worth understanding what the test work actually is. If the answer is “writing the traffic generator and the replay harness”, that is excellent and the roles are misnamed. If it is manual test cases against mock data, the ratio is wrong.

09Seven ways to do it better

Offer these as suggestions, not conditions. Most of them make their life easier too.

SuggestionWhat it buys
1 · Thin slice instead of a display POCOne real device, through their listener, into a store, onto one screen showing engine hours. Answers every dangerous question in five weeks instead of thirteen, and it is a more honest demo.
2 · Build the traffic generator firstUnblocks their entire schedule from our procurement. Also the only practical way to test replay, duplicates and clock skew.
3 · Adopt the protocol parserCodec 8E parsing is solved and field-tested by open-source projects. Reimplementing it is weeks of work and most of the protocol risk, for a benefit that should be named explicitly.
4 · Reuse the seven screensThey exist, on brand, with the data shapes modelled. Wiring them to live data is integration work, not design work.
5 · Start smaller, ramp on evidenceThree people on the thin slice; grow when there is parallel work. Reduces their hiring risk and gets to first real data sooner.
6 · Fix the record shape early, and keep it oursOne agreed telemetry record that everything downstream consumes. It is the contract between our track and theirs, and it is what lets either side change tools later without a rewrite.
7 · Demo against real or generated data, weeklyNot status reports. A running system every week, fed by awkward data, is the only progress signal that cannot be faked.

10A different shape

Same duration, same team, one difference: when you find out. The commercial version of this is straightforward and standard — commit to the first six weeks, not to the five months. A fixed-price, fixed-deliverable thin slice, followed by a re-estimate grounded in real work.

What the first six weeks should deliver

  • A listener that terminates a real device, completes the handshake and acknowledges correctly
  • The agreed telemetry record shape, and a traffic generator that produces it
  • Two test units reporting from a bench, and one adapter fitted to one real machine
  • Engine hours from that machine visible on one screen
  • A re-estimate for the rest, written by people who have now seen the data

If a team cannot do that in six weeks with three people, the five-month plan was never going to hold. If they can, you have a partner and an estimate you can trust.

11The seam between us

Three things to agree in writing, whoever builds what. None of them is contentious, and all three are usually left implicit until they cause a problem.

  • The telemetry record shape is jointly agreed and stays ours. It is the interface between their software and our devices. Given away, it becomes their internal format and every future change needs them.
  • “Installed” means engine hours observed on the server. Not “device fitted”. A device reporting position but no engine hours looks perfectly healthy, and the difference is only visible on their side — so the definition has to be shared or the failure is found months later.
  • A dated dependency list, maintained by both sides. Section 06 is the starting version. It takes ten minutes a week and it is the single most effective protection against the schedule quietly slipping at the handoffs.

12Draft message

Send as-is or trim. It asks for the three things that matter, offers something useful, and does not open a negotiation about headcount.

Thanks for the roadmap — helpful to see the shape of it. Before we go further I have a few questions, and a couple of suggestions that might make things easier on your side too.

On the estimate. Could you break the five months down into rough person-months by area — protocol and ingestion, data model and access control, dashboard screens, reporting, infrastructure, testing? Not to the day, just enough that we can see where the effort sits. Related: does the five months start at signature or once the team is fully hired, and what is the plan if hiring runs longer than expected?

On the split. We're handling the hardware side — Teltonika, the CAN adapters, SIMs, procurement and installation. That means there are handoffs between us that neither plan currently dates, and I'd rather we work them out now than discover them later. Specifically:

  • What do you need from us, and by when? Test devices, SIMs, a machine to fit an adapter to, the fleet list?
  • When can you give us an endpoint to configure devices against? We can't ship a unit before that exists.
  • We'll need a working listener before installs start — otherwise there's no way to verify a fit actually worked.

On approach. A few things worth discussing:

  1. The devices speak Teltonika's binary Codec 8E over raw TCP — no MQTT, no HTTPS — and something has to complete the IMEI handshake and return the acknowledgement. Are you planning to write that parser, or adopt an existing one? Open-source servers already do this in production.
  2. Rather than a display POC in month one, would a thin end-to-end slice work better — one real device through your listener into a store, with engine hours on one screen? It answers the risky questions early and gives us both a real demo. We can have test units on a bench quickly.
  3. We already have seven dashboard screens built as a working prototype, plus the architecture and protocol documents. Worth a look before scoping the POC — some of it may already be done.
  4. A traffic generator that produces valid Codec 8E frames would let you work at full speed while our procurement runs, and it's the only practical way to test what happens when a device reconnects after hours offline. Is that in the plan?

One technical question I'd like your view on: when your server acknowledges a batch, has it already been durably stored? The device deletes its only copy on acknowledgement, and this data is what our utilisation records are built from, so the ordering matters to us more than usual.

Happy to go through all of this on a call. And a genuine question rather than a leading one: what would you cut, and what would you need from us, to have something real running in eight weeks?

The point of all this

Not to win an argument about headcount. It is to find out whether they have understood that this system involves physical machines on sites, with intermittent connectivity, whose data ends up in a record someone relies on months later. A team that gets that will produce a better plan on their own once the questions are in front of them — and a team that does not will produce the same number again in different words.
