Telematics Supplier Shortlist

Where to look for people who can actually build this — platform vendors, engineering firms and specialists in India and elsewhere — and how to tell them apart from the firms that will quote nine people for it.

00How to read this

These are starting points, not recommendations

I have not worked with any of these companies and cannot vouch for any of them. Everything below is assembled from public sources — company sites, directories and industry listings — all of which are marketing. Treat the names as a place to begin a search, and the screening questions in sections 07 to 09 as the part that actually does the work.

The most useful thing here is probably not the list. It is the observation in section 01, and the technical screen in section 08.

01The prior question

Before hiring anyone to build a telematics backend, it is worth being clear that this is a market with mature products in it. Terminating Codec 8E, managing a device registry, decoding CAN parameters and storing telemetry is not novel work — several companies sell it as a service, and Teltonika devices are among the most widely supported hardware in that market.

  • flespi terminates Teltonika protocols directly and exposes the decoded stream over an API — it exists precisely to remove the parser problem, and Teltonika documents it as a supported route.
  • Wialon and Gurtam run one of the largest telematics platforms in the world, with a partner network of over 2,700 integrators.
  • Several Indian companies sell white-label platforms outright, which is a different purchase again.

The question worth asking before the hiring question

What does building this ourselves buy that buying it does not? There are legitimate answers — owning the evidence path end to end, controlling the acknowledgement, holding the data and the tenancy model, avoiding a per-device fee that scales with the fleet. But the answer should be stated, because the alternative to a five-month build is not a longer build, it is a subscription and an integration.

A middle path is available and probably the strongest: buy the ingestion, own the model. Let a platform or an adopted server terminate the devices, and keep the telemetry contract, the tenancy model and the evidence store in-house. That is the same reversibility rule the architecture already argues for, applied to procurement.

02Three supplier markets

The firms in the third box are numerous, well-marketed, and dominate search results for “fleet management software development company”. They build good web applications. They are usually not the right people to hand a binary protocol, a CAN bus and an acknowledgement-durability requirement — and a proposal that treats the whole thing as a dashboard project is the signature of that mismatch.

04Engineering firms

For the case where the ingestion tier, CAN decoding and the data model are genuinely being built.

Embedded / automotive engineering

The right shape of firm for device-protocol and CAN work. Smaller market, harder to search for, worth the effort.

Embitel Technologies (Bangalore, with a UAE presence) is the clearest example found: automotive and embedded product engineering, telematics control units, CAN and J1939 work, and published AIS-140 stack development. Their case studies are about protocol stacks and control units rather than dashboards, which is the distinction that matters here.

Look for the same signature elsewhere: automotive Tier-1 clients, ECU or TCU work, CAN and J1939 named explicitly, and case studies about protocols rather than screens.

Telematics product companies with services arms

Companies that run their own platforms and also take custom work. They have already solved the hard parts for themselves.

Uffizio, TrackoBit, Watsoo and Sensel Telematics all operate in this space in India. The advantage is obvious — they have terminated device protocols in production. The thing to check is whether they will build something you own, or only resell their own platform with a different logo.

General application development firms

Numerous, heavily marketed, and the default result of any search in this area.

Industry listicles of “top fleet management software development companies in India” are dominated by large general app-dev houses — this list is a representative example, naming firms of 200 to 1,500 people in Jaipur, Ahmedabad, Noida, Pune and Gurugram. Several are competent at what they do. But these lists are SEO placements rather than assessments, and none of the entries demonstrates device-protocol work.

This is the category that produces a nine-person, five-month quote for a ten-device pilot. They are appropriate for the screens, the reporting and the API — after someone else has solved the ingestion.

05Specialists and freelancers

For a project of this size, one experienced telematics engineer is worth more than a team of five generalists — and easier to find than the market suggests, because the people who have done this are visible.

  • Open-source contributors. Teltonika Codec 8 and 8E parsers exist publicly in several languages — for example filipkroca/teltonikaparser and a Codec 8E TCP server, and the Teltonika topic collects more. The people who wrote and maintain these have demonstrably done the exact work, which is a stronger signal than any proposal. Several take contract work; many are in Eastern Europe and Latin America rather than India.
  • The Traccar community. Contributors who have added or fixed device protocols in Traccar are, by definition, people who have debugged real device traffic.
  • Marketplaces. Freelancer and Upwork both carry Traccar and Teltonika work; quality is highly variable and the screen in section 08 matters more here than anywhere else.

A cheap, high-signal first engagement

Pay one specialist for two weeks to stand up an ingestion path against two real devices on a bench and report what the hardware actually does. It costs a fraction of a month of a nine-person team, it answers the open protocol questions directly, and it tells you more about whether someone can do this work than any interview will. Run it with two candidates in parallel if the choice is close.

07The AIS-140 filter

The most useful India-specific screening test found, and worth knowing about.

AIS-140 is India's mandatory standard for vehicle tracking on commercial and public service vehicles. Meeting it requires real device-protocol work: binary packet handling, device provisioning, prescribed message formats, and integration with government backend systems. It is not a dashboard exercise, and it is externally verified.

How to use it

Ask any Indian candidate: have you built or integrated an AIS-140 compliant backend, and can you talk me through the packet structure? A firm that has done it will answer immediately and in detail. A firm that has not will pivot to describing their fleet dashboard. It is a fast, non-confrontational way to separate the two markets in section 02, and it works on the phone.

08Technical screen

Six questions. Any candidate — company or individual — who cannot answer these has not done this work, whatever their proposal says.

AskWhat a real answer sounds like
Walk me through a Codec 8E session from TCP connect to the device clearing its buffer.IMEI handshake, single-byte accept or reject, the AVL packet, CRC validation, the four-byte record-count acknowledgement. Immediate and unprompted.
When do you send the acknowledgement?The most revealing question in the list. A real answer engages with acknowledging only after the record is durably stored, and why acknowledging early loses data silently.
A device reconnects after four hours offline and replays its buffer. What breaks?Out-of-order timestamps, duplicate records, trip reconstruction, stale alerts firing. Someone who has lived through it lists these without thinking.
How do you get engine hours off a machine that is not a truck?CAN adapter, J1939, and — critically — that support depends on a program number for that specific make, model and year. If they think engine hours are simply available, they have never done it.
How do you test before hardware arrives?A traffic generator built from the protocol specification. If the answer is “we wait for devices”, the schedule has a hole in it.
What does a completed installation mean?Data verified as arriving on the server — including engine hours — before sign-off. Anything less means the failures surface months later.

09Red flags

  • A proposal with no hardware, procurement or installation in it. The most reliable single indicator that a supplier has not built a telematics system before.
  • Team size quoted before scope is agreed. Headcount arriving ahead of a written scope means the number came from a staffing template.
  • A first milestone that is a UI. It defers every question that could invalidate the design.
  • “We will need to recruit for this.” Not disqualifying, but it means you are funding a team build and carrying the hiring risk. Price it accordingly.
  • Vagueness about who owns the data model and the code. Establish before signing that the telemetry contract and the schema are yours.
  • No questions back. A supplier who has done this before will ask about machine makes and models, reporting interval, fleet size and where the data must live. One who quotes without asking is quoting a template.

10How to engage

  1. Write the scope first. One page: ten machines, FMC130 with CAN adapter, these capabilities, this data ownership requirement. Send the same page to everyone. Differences in the responses become meaningful rather than noise.
  2. Buy a paid discovery before a build. Two to four weeks, fixed price, delivering an ingestion path against two real devices and a written report on what the hardware actually does. Cheap, and it converts sales conversations into evidence.
  3. Run two in parallel if the choice is close. At discovery scale the cost of doing this twice is far below the cost of choosing wrong for five months.
  4. Never let the first phase be the whole commitment. Contract the discovery, then the pilot, then scale — with the option to change supplier at each boundary.
  5. Keep the contract, the schema and the evidence store in-house whoever builds the ingestion. That is the same reversibility rule the architecture argues for, and it is what makes changing supplier survivable.
